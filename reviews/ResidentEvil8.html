Resident Evil Village

✅ RECOMMENDED

8/10

A beautifully atmospheric and surprisingly well-paced horror adventure filled with memorable villains, immense locations and one deeply upsetting nightmare baby.

  • Hours Played Overall: 13.8 Hours

Should You Play In 2026?

Survival horror isn't normally my thing. I'm not someone who actively seeks out games designed to make me deeply uncomfortable while wandering around with limited ammunition and a protagonist who sometimes appears determined to make every bad situation considerably worse. Despite that, I honestly really enjoyed Resident Evil Village.

What surprised me most was how well paced the whole experience felt. There are quieter moments where I could explore and get my bearings, intense encounters that put pressure on my resources and sections that are just genuinely horrifying. The game moves between these different styles effectively, preventing the horror from becoming exhausting without letting me feel completely safe for too long.

The story was also much better than I expected. I hadn't played Resident Evil 7 beforehand, so I didn't have the full context for everything involving Ethan and his family. However, Village gave me enough information to understand what was happening and become invested in the story. I never felt like I needed to stop playing and research the previous game just to follow the main events.

I really liked Ethan as a protagonist, even if the man is occasionally DUMB AS DIRT. His decision to provoke Moreau was a particularly baffling moment. Mate, you're already having a terrible day. Maybe stop actively making it worse? He has an impressive ability to unintentionally create even more problems for himself, and there were several moments when I wanted to reach through the screen and shake some common sense into him.

The Four Lords were one of my favourite parts of the story. They are equally intimidating and awe-inspiring, but each has a completely different personality and presence. Lady Dimitrescu was fantastic, while Heisenberg was ridiculously charismatic. I definitely developed a bit of a crush on him, which probably says something about my judgement in fictional men. Mother Miranda, meanwhile, is completely fucking crazy.

I also loved how the story eventually connected back to the very beginning of the series. Even without the full context of the previous game, seeing how everything tied together was pretty freaking cool.

The Duke became my emotional support blanket for most of the game. Whenever I found him, I knew I could relax for approximately five minutes before the game inevitably found another way to traumatise me. Those moments of safety were especially welcome because every major location has its own distinctive atmosphere and variety of threats.

Castle Dimitrescu is absolutely beautiful while still being deeply unsettling. Exploring its rooms and corridors was intimidating enough, but Lady Dimitrescu stalking around made the whole experience considerably more stressful. There were several occasions when I opened a door only for the massive lady herself to come straight through it, immediately resulting in me turning around and running in the opposite direction.

Then there's House Beneviento.

House Beneviento can officially go die in a fire.

This was easily the scariest part of the entire game for me. A house full of dolls was already pushing things too far, but then the baby monster appeared and the situation moved firmly into absolutely fucking not territory. The combination of the house's oppressive atmosphere, the dolls and that deeply upsetting creature genuinely messed me up for a while. Congratulations, Resident Evil Village. Mission accomplished. I hated it.

Complimentary.

Mostly.

The reservoir is suitably grimy and unpleasant, while everything leading towards Heisenberg becomes increasingly brutal. The Stronghold absolutely murdered my ammunition because of its nonstop fights, but it also created a memorable sense of desperation. Heisenberg's Factory then finished things off with a massive and incredibly atmospheric location that I loved exploring.

Heisenberg was also my favourite boss. The material used for the tank felt like a slight MacGuffin, but I enjoyed the resulting encounter enough that I was willing to roll with it. It helped that Heisenberg had already established himself as one of the most entertaining and charismatic characters in the game.

Outside of House Beneviento, I found the horror surprisingly manageable. Castle Dimitrescu remained intimidating because I never knew when Lady Dimitrescu might appear, and there were a handful of jumpscares, but nothing else felt too drastic. From what I've seen of Resident Evil 7, that game certainly appears scarier, although I haven't played it myself and therefore can't make a direct comparison.

Would I recommend Resident Evil Village in 2026? Definitely. It managed to get me invested in a genre that I'm not normally drawn to, which probably says more than anything else. I enjoyed the story, loved the villains and locations, became emotionally dependent on the Duke and discovered that my horror tolerance ends somewhere around "giant nightmare baby in creepy doll house".

What I Loved

  • Excellent pacing between exploration, action and genuine horror.
  • The Four Lords are memorable, intimidating and completely different from one another.
  • Each major location has its own distinctive and powerful atmosphere.
  • Castle Dimitrescu is beautiful, haunting and constantly intimidating.
  • House Beneviento is genuinely horrifying and left a lasting impression.
  • Heisenberg is ridiculously charismatic and gets my favourite boss fight.
  • The Duke is my emotional support blanket, and I will hear nothing against him.

What Didn't Work

  • Ethan is occasionally DUMB AS DIRT and seems determined to make difficult situations even worse.
  • The Stronghold's nonstop fighting absolutely murdered my ammunition.
  • The material used for Heisenberg's tank felt like a slight MacGuffin.

Favourite Moment

My favourite moment had to be the section with Chris near the end of the game. After spending most of Resident Evil Village carefully managing limited resources and constantly worrying about how much ammunition I had left, suddenly being handed an absolute arsenal was an incredible change of pace. It was basically a pure power trip, and after everything the game had put me through up to that point, it felt immensely satisfying to finally be the biggest threat in the room.

Favourite Boss / Character

Heisenberg was my favourite boss and one of my favourite characters. I found him incredibly charismatic, and his personality made every scene involving him entertaining. His final encounter also stood out as my favourite boss fight, even if the material used to create the tank was a convenient MacGuffin.

Who Would Enjoy This?

Resident Evil Village should appeal to players who enjoy horror games but don't necessarily want relentless terror from beginning to end. Its mixture of atmospheric exploration, intense fights, memorable villains and genuinely horrifying set pieces gives the experience plenty of variety. It may also work well for players who aren't usually drawn to survival horror, because that combination was enough to win me over.

Steam Clean Verdict

✅ RECOMMENDED

Resident Evil Village is atmospheric, ridiculous, horrifying when it wants to be and genuinely good fun. Its memorable villains and wildly distinctive locations kept me invested, while its pacing made the horror manageable without removing its impact. I may never forgive House Beneviento, but I had a bloody good time everywhere else.
