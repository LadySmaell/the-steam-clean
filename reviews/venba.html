Should You Play In 2026?

Yes. In fact, I'd go a step further and say if you've got a spare evening and want something genuinely heartfelt, Venba deserves a place near the top of your backlog.

At roughly two hours long, Venba understands the value of brevity. It never drags things out, never overstays its welcome and never feels like it is trying to pad out its runtime. Instead, it tells a focused story about family, culture and connection that manages to be surprisingly powerful despite its short length.

The first thing that struck me was how beautiful the game is. The art style is wonderful throughout, creating a warm and inviting atmosphere that perfectly suits the story being told. Combined with an excellent soundtrack, Venba immediately feels comforting. It's one of those games that wraps itself around you like a warm blanket and asks you to slow down for a while.

The story itself is where the game shines brightest. While the themes explored are rooted in a specific culture and family experience, the emotions at the heart of the narrative are incredibly relatable. As an adult, and particularly as a parent, there were several moments that hit surprisingly hard. The game tackles relationships, misunderstandings and the passage of time in a way that feels authentic without becoming overly dramatic.

One of the most enjoyable aspects of the experience was learning about Tamil and Indian cooking. Food isn't just a gameplay mechanic here; it's an important part of the storytelling. I genuinely enjoyed piecing together recipes and learning a little more about a culture I wasn't particularly familiar with beforehand. It's educational without ever feeling like it's trying to teach a lesson.

Gameplay is intentionally simple. Most of the interaction revolves around preparing dishes and progressing the narrative through light puzzle solving. I can absolutely see some players wanting more from a mechanical perspective, and if you're looking for deep systems or challenging gameplay, Venba probably won't scratch that itch. Personally, though, I found the simplicity refreshing. It acts as an excellent palate cleanser between larger, more demanding games and allows the story to remain the focus.

The only real criticism I have is one that's less of a flaw and more of a consideration. The gameplay won't be for everyone, simply because there isn't a huge amount of complexity to it. Beyond that, there was only one other issue: this game made me incredibly hungry. Looking at delicious food for two hours is not conducive to sensible snacking decisions.

What impressed me most is how much Venba manages to accomplish in such a short runtime. It tells a complete story, introduces memorable characters, celebrates a culture through food and leaves a lasting emotional impact without a single wasted moment.

Just play it. It's short, sweet and tells a beautiful story with more heart than many games ten times its length.

Favourite Moment

The ending. Without spoiling anything, seeing two characters finally have the conversation they'd needed for a long time was a genuinely touching moment.

Favourite Boss / Character

Venba. Despite coming from completely different backgrounds, there were countless moments as a parent where I completely understood her perspective.

Who Would Enjoy This?

Players looking for a meaningful narrative experience, fans of story-driven indie games and anyone wanting something shorter between larger gaming commitments. If you've ever appreciated games that can tell a powerful story in just a couple of hours, Venba is well worth your time.
