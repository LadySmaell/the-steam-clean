STEAM CLEAN REVIEW

Selfie Worm

✅ RECOMMENDED

8/10

A deceptively simple physics puzzle game that hides one of the most heartfelt and bittersweet stories I've experienced in a casual game.

  • Hours Played Overall: 4.8 Hours
  • Achievement Progress: 20/23
  • Steam Clean Size: QUICK

Should You Play In 2026?

Absolutely. Selfie Worm looks like a light-hearted physics game about taking funny photographs with an adorable worm, but beneath its colourful smartphone-inspired presentation is a genuinely moving story about family, loss and carrying on someone's legacy.

The core gameplay is incredibly simple. You control your worm using only the left and right mouse buttons, bouncing and wriggling your way onto objects so a selfie can be taken. That simplicity makes the game immediately approachable and means almost anyone can pick it up and play. Even better, the game understands that some players will want a little more challenge, which is where the optional to-do lists come in. Completing specific requirements on every photograph can be surprisingly demanding and gave me plenty of reasons to revisit stages after I'd already completed them.

Visually, the game is charming. Everything is designed to look like an actual smartphone screen, complete with apps and menus, helping the game feel like you're exploring somebody's device rather than navigating traditional menus. The bright colours and cheerful presentation make the experience feel welcoming from start to finish.

There is also far more content here than I expected. Alongside the main photography challenges, there is a surprisingly enjoyable pinball minigame that lets you unlock cosmetic skins and headwear for your worm. I had far more fun with this than I care to admit. The corrupted files provide a second distraction in the form of slider puzzles which unlock photographs tied to Annie and her father throughout different points in their lives. Thankfully these puzzles never become frustratingly difficult, but they add enough variety to keep things fresh while contributing to the wider narrative.

The soundtrack deserves special praise as well. There are plenty of unlockable music tracks available throughout the game, giving you lots of options to customise the atmosphere. While I enjoyed the wider soundtrack, the title theme remained my personal favourite. It's absolutely adorable and perfectly captures the game's personality.

What truly makes Selfie Worm special, however, is its story. Through text messages, corrupted files and photographs, the game gradually reveals the life of Annie and her widowed father. What initially feels like a quirky collection of smartphone apps slowly develops into something much more emotional as her father becomes ill with leukaemia and rushes to finish the game before running out of time.

The narrative builds towards a wonderfully bittersweet conclusion centred around an unfinished promise to visit Uluru together. The final puzzle depicts Uluru itself, and completing it reveals a grown-up Annie making that journey with a letter left behind by her father. Later she writes back to him, explaining that she found the person she wanted to walk through life with, fulfilled the dreams he had for her and even finished the game he could not complete himself. The final image of Annie's wedding day was the emotional knockout punch that completely caught me off guard.

Honestly, I struggle to find many faults worth mentioning. Selfie Worm is exactly what it sets out to be: a short, accessible and surprisingly heartfelt experience. Some of the optional challenges can be frustrating when chasing perfect completion times or specific objectives, but even when they were driving me up the wall I was still having fun.

If you're looking for a relaxing game with simple mechanics, plenty of collectibles and a story that might leave you reaching for a tissue by the end, Selfie Worm is absolutely worth your time.

What I Loved

  • A genuinely emotional story that completely exceeded my expectations.
  • Simple, accessible controls that make it easy for anyone to play.
  • Excellent smartphone-inspired presentation and colourful visual style.
  • Fun side activities including pinball, collectibles and narrative slider puzzles.

What Didn't Work

  • Some optional challenge objectives can become surprisingly difficult.
  • Honestly, I struggled to find any major issues beyond the occasional completionist frustration.

Favourite Moment

Completing the final Uluru puzzle and seeing the reveal of Annie as an adult carrying her father's letter. It transformed what had been a charming puzzle game into something truly memorable and made the ending hit far harder than I expected.

Favourite Boss / Character

Annie's father. Despite never becoming larger than life, his determination to finish the game and leave something meaningful behind for his daughter forms the emotional heart of the entire experience.

Who Would Enjoy This?

Players who enjoy cosy games, casual puzzle experiences, desktop companion style games and narrative-driven stories. It's especially easy to recommend to anyone who likes finding surprisingly emotional stories hidden beneath simple gameplay mechanics.
