Selfie Worm

✅ RECOMMENDED

8/10

A deceptively simple physics puzzle game that hides one of the most heartfelt and memorable stories I've encountered in a casual game.

  • Completed: 22/10/2026
  • Hours Played Overall: 4.8
  • Achievement Progress: 20/23
  • Steam Clean Size: QUICK

Should You Play In 2026?

Absolutely. Selfie Worm initially presents itself as a simple and colourful physics puzzle game, but it quickly proves to be much more than that. The core objective is straightforward: use the left and right mouse buttons to guide your worm into position for the perfect selfie. It's easy to understand, accessible to almost anyone and immediately enjoyable.

While the controls are simple, there is still plenty to master. Players looking for a more relaxed experience can simply enjoy collecting photos and progressing through the game, while completionists can tackle the optional to-do list challenges. Some of these objectives genuinely surprised me with how tricky they were and gave me plenty of reason to revisit levels after clearing them for the first time.

The game's presentation is fantastic. Everything is designed to resemble a real smartphone, complete with apps, menus and unlockables. The bright visuals give the game plenty of personality, while the wide range of unlockable music helps make the experience feel surprisingly cosy. There are also a number of side activities, including a surprisingly addictive pinball game and slider-puzzle style challenges that help break up the main gameplay.

Speaking of music, there is a healthy amount to unlock throughout the game. While I enjoyed the soundtrack in general, the title theme was easily my favourite. It's absolutely adorable and perfectly captures the game's warm and welcoming atmosphere.

What really elevates Selfie Worm above similar casual games is the narrative running quietly in the background. Through messages, photographs and hidden discoveries, the game tells a surprisingly heartfelt story that unfolds naturally as you play. I won't spoil any of the details, but I can safely say it left a much bigger impression on me than I expected when I first launched what appeared to be a cute game about taking worm selfies.

It's rare for a game with such simple mechanics to deliver such a memorable emotional experience, but Selfie Worm manages it. Whether you're looking for a relaxing game to dip into throughout the day or a charming story that stays with you after the credits roll, this is well worth your time.

I honestly struggled to find much to dislike. Even when some of the optional objectives were pushing my patience, I was still having fun. Selfie Worm knows exactly what it wants to be and executes that vision incredibly well.

What I Loved

  • A surprisingly emotional narrative that stayed with me long after I finished playing.
  • Simple, accessible controls that make the game easy for anyone to pick up.
  • A fantastic smartphone-inspired presentation filled with charm and personality.
  • Great side content including pinball, collectibles and puzzle challenges that add variety.

What Didn't Work

  • Some optional challenge objectives can be surprisingly demanding when chasing full completion.
  • I genuinely struggled to find any major issues beyond occasional completionist frustration.

Favourite Moment

The moment when all of the discoveries you've been piecing together throughout the game finally click into place. It's handled with a surprising amount of subtlety and gave the game's emotional themes far more impact than I was expecting.

Favourite Boss / Character

Annie. Even though much of her story is told indirectly, she remains the emotional centre of the experience and gives the narrative its heart.

Who Would Enjoy This?

Fans of cosy games, casual physics puzzles, desktop companion-style experiences and narrative-driven adventures. If you enjoy games that look light-hearted on the surface but have more depth than you initially expect, Selfie Worm is an easy recommendation.
