Selfie Worm Review

Score: 8/10

Verdict: ✅ RECOMMENDED

Short Summary

Selfie Worm is a charming physics-based casual game that initially looks like a colourful desktop distraction, but quietly delivers a surprisingly emotional story underneath its bright exterior. With accessible gameplay, plenty of optional challenges, enjoyable side activities and a genuinely heartfelt narrative, it left a much bigger impression on me than I ever expected.

The Review

On the surface, Selfie Worm is a simple physics game about moving a worm into position so a photo can be taken. Using nothing more than the left and right mouse buttons, you bounce and wriggle your way across a variety of objects, trying to settle in just the right place before snapping the perfect shot. It is incredibly easy to understand, making it the kind of game that almost anyone can pick up and enjoy within minutes.

That simplicity is one of the game's greatest strengths. There is virtually no skill barrier to entry, and the basic mechanics remain enjoyable throughout. However, for players who want something a little more demanding, the optional to-do list challenges add some genuine difficulty. Certain photo objectives require very specific setups or efficient runs, and I will happily admit that more than a few of them absolutely kicked my backside before I finally completed them.

Visually, Selfie Worm is delightful. The entire game is presented as though you are interacting with a real smartphone, which gives it an identity that feels both familiar and surprisingly immersive. The bright colours and cheerful presentation create an inviting atmosphere from the very beginning, helping the game maintain its light-hearted charm even when some of its deeper themes begin to emerge.

There is also a surprising amount of content packed into what initially appears to be a straightforward casual experience. Alongside the main photography gameplay, there are several additional activities to dive into. The pinball minigame allows you to unlock new skins and headwear for your worm and, if I am being honest, I probably spent more time enjoying that than I expected to. It is simple, addictive and a fun reward system rolled into one.

Then there are the corrupted files, which take the form of slider puzzles. Thankfully these never become excessively difficult, but they provide a welcome change of pace. Completing them unlocks photographs that gradually reveal more of the game's narrative. Some are happy, some are heartbreaking, but every single one contributes something meaningful to the larger story being told.

The smartphone presentation extends beyond the main game too. Standard apps such as a calculator, music player and even a focus timer are all included. The focus timer is a particularly nice touch if you simply want to relax for a while, and it reinforces the feeling that Selfie Worm sits comfortably alongside the growing number of desktop companion and idle-style experiences.

The music deserves special mention as well. There is a healthy selection of tracks to unlock throughout the game, giving players plenty of choice when it comes to the soundtrack accompanying their worm-based photography adventures. While there are several great options available, the title theme remained my personal favourite. It is absolutely adorable and perfectly captures the game's warm and welcoming personality.

What truly elevates Selfie Worm, however, is its story. Hidden beneath the cheerful visuals and simple gameplay is a bittersweet tale about Annie and her father. Through text messages, photographs and corrupted files, the game gradually reveals the life of a widowed father raising his daughter while developing a game based on a character she created.

As the narrative progresses, the tone becomes increasingly emotional. Annie's father develops leukaemia and begins racing against time to finish the project, leaving behind unfinished dreams and promises, including a planned trip to Uluru. The final puzzle centres around Uluru itself, and completing it reveals a grown-up Annie finally making that journey with a letter from her father in hand.

The story's conclusion hit me far harder than I was expecting. Annie later writes a response to her father, explaining that she found the person she wanted to share her life with, fulfilled his wish that she would not have to walk alone, and even managed to finish the game while fixing all the remaining bugs. The final image showing Annie on her wedding day serves as a beautiful and emotional closing note. It is bittersweet, heartfelt and handled with remarkable restraint.

Honestly, I struggled to find much to dislike here. Selfie Worm knows exactly what it wants to be. It is a short, casual experience with accessible gameplay, but it also delivers a genuinely touching story that stayed with me long after the credits rolled. Even when certain challenge requirements tested my patience, I was always having a good time.

What I Liked

  • Surprisingly emotional and well-told narrative.
  • Simple controls make it accessible to virtually everyone.
  • Optional challenges add difficulty for completionists.
  • Fantastic smartphone-inspired presentation and aesthetic.
  • Fun side activities including pinball and slider puzzles.
  • Excellent soundtrack with lots of unlockable music.
  • Plenty of reasons to keep playing for 100% completion.

What I Didn't Like

  • I genuinely struggled to find any major faults worth mentioning.

Final Verdict

Selfie Worm may look like a simple physics-based photography game, but beneath its cheerful smartphone aesthetic lies a touching story about family, loss and carrying someone's legacy forward. The accessible gameplay, enjoyable minigames, charming presentation and unexpectedly powerful narrative combine to create a memorable experience that lingers long after the final puzzle is completed.

It is short, sweet, occasionally challenging and far more emotional than its premise suggests.

8/10 - ✅ RECOMMENDED
