Killer Frequency Review

⭐ OUTSTANDING

Killer Frequency combines incredible aesthetics, fantastic voice acting, an engaging story, and genuine tension to create a memorable horror experience. A few pacing issues and some occasionally obtuse puzzles stop it from reaching perfection, but it remains an easy recommendation.

Introduction

There are plenty of horror games that rely on jump scares and relentless time pressure to keep players on edge. Killer Frequency takes a different approach. Instead of constantly rushing the player, it builds tension through atmosphere, strong writing, and the feeling that every decision carries real consequences.

Across my roughly six-hour playthrough, I found myself consistently invested in both the story and the people caught up in it. It is a game that understands how to create dread without exhausting the player, resulting in an experience that is tense, engaging, and surprisingly difficult to put down.

The Review

From the moment I started playing, the presentation immediately stood out. Killer Frequency has an incredible aesthetic that gives it a unique identity and helps establish its atmosphere from the outset. Every environment feels purposeful, and the game makes excellent use of its surroundings to create tension without resorting to cheap tricks or endless darkness.

That atmosphere is reinforced by an excellent soundtrack. The music consistently complements the action on screen, helping to build suspense during stressful moments while also enhancing quieter sections. Rather than simply existing in the background, the soundtrack becomes an important part of what makes the game so immersive.

What surprised me most was how invested I became in the story. The narrative remains engaging throughout and, more importantly, the stakes genuinely feel real. I cared about what happened next, and the game did an excellent job of making decisions feel significant. That connection is strengthened by a cast of well-written and likeable characters who are easy to become attached to as the story unfolds.

The voice acting deserves special recognition. Every performance feels natural and believable, bringing both the story and characters to life. In a game that relies so heavily on dialogue and tension, weak voice work could have easily undermined the experience. Instead, Killer Frequency delivers some genuinely excellent performances that elevate the entire game.

Gameplay itself is straightforward, falling firmly into the category of a first-person spooky puzzler. Exploration and puzzle solving form the core of the experience, and for the most part I appreciated how the game maintained a strong sense of tension without constantly imposing strict time limits. Despite the high stakes, I rarely felt pressured into rushing through sections, which allowed me to soak in the atmosphere and enjoy the story at my own pace.

That said, the game is not without flaws. The pacing occasionally slows down a little too much, causing some sections to drag more than they probably should. There were also a handful of puzzles that felt slightly obtuse, with solutions that were not always as intuitive as the game seemed to expect.

Replayability presents another minor issue. Players chasing achievements may find the lack of a chapter select feature frustrating. Being forced into multiple full playthroughs to clean up specific achievements feels more inconvenient than rewarding, especially when individual sections cannot be revisited directly.

Technically, however, I have very few complaints. The game ran beautifully throughout my entire playthrough, ensuring the focus remained firmly on the story, atmosphere, and characters rather than any performance issues.

Positives

  • Incredible aesthetic and visual presentation
  • Excellent soundtrack
  • Engaging story with genuine stakes
  • Well-written and likeable characters
  • Outstanding voice acting
  • Great use of environmental design
  • Strong feeling of dread throughout
  • Creates tension without relying heavily on time pressure
  • Ran beautifully during my playthrough

Negatives

  • Pacing occasionally feels slow
  • Some puzzle solutions can be a little obtuse
  • No chapter select for easier achievement hunting

Should You Play in ?

Absolutely. Killer Frequency succeeds because every major element works together so well. The atmosphere is excellent, the characters are genuinely enjoyable to spend time with, the voice acting is fantastic, and the story maintains a sense of tension that kept me invested throughout.

While the pacing occasionally drifts and a handful of puzzles can be mildly frustrating, neither issue comes close to overshadowing what is otherwise an excellent horror adventure. If you enjoy narrative-driven horror games that prioritise atmosphere and suspense over constant jump scares, Killer Frequency is well worth your time.

Final Verdict

⭐ OUTSTANDING

Killer Frequency delivers a compelling blend of atmosphere, storytelling, music, and character writing. Its exceptional voice acting and genuine sense of dread make it a standout horror experience, while only minor pacing and puzzle issues prevent it from achieving true greatness.

Score: 8.5/10
