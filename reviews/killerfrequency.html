Killer Frequency

⭐ OUTSTANDING 8.5/10

A brilliantly atmospheric horror puzzler with fantastic voice acting, memorable characters, and a genuine sense of dread that keeps the tension high without constantly rushing the player.

Should You Play In 2026?

Absolutely. Killer Frequency is one of those games that understands tension doesn't always need a countdown timer attached to it. Instead of constantly pushing the player forward, it builds suspense through atmosphere, strong writing and the feeling that every decision could have serious consequences. The result is a horror experience that kept me invested from beginning to end.

The game's aesthetic is fantastic and immediately sets the tone. Combined with an excellent soundtrack, Killer Frequency creates an atmosphere that constantly balances comfort and unease. There's a genuine feeling of dread hanging over much of the experience, and the environmental design does a great job of supporting that without relying on cheap scares.

The story was the biggest surprise for me. It remained engaging throughout and, more importantly, the stakes always felt real. I wanted to know what would happen next and found myself genuinely invested in the outcome. That investment is helped enormously by a cast of well-written and likeable characters who are brought to life by some truly excellent voice acting. Every performance feels natural and believable, helping the game stand out from many of its genre contemporaries.

Gameplay is fairly straightforward, falling firmly into the category of a first-person spooky puzzler. Exploration and puzzle solving form the core of the experience, and for the most part I appreciated that the game rarely imposes strict time pressures despite maintaining a strong sense of urgency. It allows you to immerse yourself in the world rather than constantly panic about a ticking clock.

That's not to say everything works perfectly. The pacing occasionally slows down more than I'd like, and a handful of puzzle solutions felt a little too obtuse for their own good. Achievement hunters may also be frustrated by the lack of a chapter select feature, forcing multiple playthroughs to mop up missed achievements.

Technically, though, I have no complaints. The game ran beautifully throughout my six-hour playthrough and never distracted from what it does best: delivering an atmospheric and engaging horror experience.

What I Loved

  • Incredible aesthetics and atmosphere
  • Fantastic soundtrack
  • Engaging story with genuine stakes
  • Well-written and likeable characters
  • Outstanding voice acting
  • Great use of environmental design
  • Creates dread without relying heavily on time pressure
  • Excellent technical performance

What Didn't Work

  • Pacing occasionally feels slow
  • Some puzzle solutions can be a little obtuse
  • No chapter select for easier achievement hunting

Favourite Moment

Realising just how invested I had become in the story and the characters. The game does an excellent job of making the stakes feel meaningful, which made several key moments far more tense than I expected.

Favourite Boss / Character

Who Would Enjoy This?

Horror fans looking for a narrative-driven experience, players who enjoy puzzle solving, and anyone wanting a spooky game that builds atmosphere and dread rather than relying solely on jump scares.
