Should You Play In 2026?

Some games set out with a clear goal in mind. Others seem to emerge from a fever dream, make absolutely no attempt to explain themselves, and somehow end up being all the better for it. Mr Sleepy Man firmly belongs in the second category.

This is one of the strangest games I've played in quite some time, and I mean that as a compliment. Throughout my time with it, I regularly found myself wondering what on earth was going on. Normally that would be a criticism, but Mr Sleepy Man embraces its weirdness so completely that confusion becomes part of the appeal. Rather than feeling random or directionless, it feels like the game is inviting you to simply sit back and enjoy the absurd ride.

The presentation does a lot of heavy lifting here. The art style is undeniably charming, giving the world a playful personality that immediately makes it stand out. Every area feels vibrant and full of character, helping create a unique atmosphere that perfectly suits the game's offbeat nature. It's unapologetically cartoony, which won't be for everyone, but I found it incredibly easy to warm to.

The writing is equally important to the experience. The humour landed consistently for me, largely because the game isn't afraid to fully commit to its bizarre ideas. It understands exactly what kind of experience it wants to deliver and leans into it with complete confidence. That confidence helps even the weirdest moments feel intentional rather than simply random.

Then there's the soundtrack. Quite frankly, it's incredible. Plenty of games have good music, but Mr Sleepy Man has the kind of soundtrack that actively elevates the entire experience. Whether supporting a joke, adding energy to exploration, or simply pulling you further into its wonderfully strange world, the music is consistently excellent and easily one of the game's standout features.

Movement is another area where the game impressed me. Getting around feels great, with a satisfying sense of control and momentum once everything clicks into place. The catch is that its movement philosophy feels very old-school. That's something I personally enjoyed, but players who are more familiar with modern platforming and movement systems may find there is a learning curve. The controls themselves are good, but they ask you to adapt to a more retro style of game design.

What ultimately makes Mr Sleepy Man memorable, though, is how refreshingly unique it feels. In an era where many games can start to blur together, this one carves out its own identity through sheer personality. It doesn't feel like it's chasing trends or trying to be the next big thing. Instead, it is completely comfortable being weird, funny and occasionally difficult to explain to anyone who hasn't played it.

That uniqueness does come with a warning. The same qualities that made me enjoy it may also be barriers for some players. The relentless weirdness, cartoon-inspired aesthetic and retro-inspired movement won't appeal to everyone. If you're looking for something grounded and conventional, this probably isn't the game for you. However, if you're willing to embrace something genuinely different, Mr Sleepy Man has a lot to offer.
