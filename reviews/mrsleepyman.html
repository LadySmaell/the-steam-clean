Should You Play In 2026?

Some games are easy to explain. You can point at a genre, mention a couple of mechanics and people immediately understand what they're getting themselves into. Mr Sleepy Man is not one of those games.

This is the sort of game that regularly left me staring at the screen wondering what on earth was going on, only for me to immediately carry on because I needed to see what bizarre thing would happen next. It fully commits to its strange ideas and never once feels embarrassed about how weird it is. In fact, the weird is very much the point.

The first thing that stands out is the art style. Mr Sleepy Man has a genuinely charming visual identity that helps everything feel playful and inviting, even when the game is busy throwing another completely bonkers idea at the player. The cartoon-inspired presentation won't appeal to everyone, but it gives the game a personality that many bigger releases would kill for.

The writing is another major highlight. The humour landed consistently because the game understands exactly what kind of experience it wants to be. Rather than trying to explain every oddball concept, it embraces the absurdity and trusts the player to come along for the ride. The result is a game that feels funny, confident and completely comfortable in its own skin.

Then there's the soundtrack. Quite frankly, it's incredible. Good music can improve a game, but this soundtrack feels like an essential part of the overall experience. Whether you're exploring, platforming or simply trying to process whatever strange thing you've just witnessed, the music constantly elevates the atmosphere and keeps the adventure moving forward.

As a platformer, Mr Sleepy Man is surprisingly satisfying to play. The movement feels great and there is a genuine sense of momentum once you get comfortable with it. It carries a very retro design philosophy, which I personally enjoyed, but newer players may need a little time to adapt. The controls aren't bad by any means, they're simply built around an older style of movement that rewards a bit of patience.

What ultimately makes the game memorable is how unique it feels. In an industry where many games can start to blur together, this one confidently does its own thing. It doesn't feel interested in chasing trends or fitting neatly into a category. Instead, it focuses on delivering a genuinely unusual adventure full of personality, humour and imagination.

That uniqueness inevitably comes with caveats. Some players may find the game's relentless weirdness a little too much, particularly if they prefer more focused storytelling or traditional world building. Likewise, the retro-inspired movement and cartoon aesthetic may not click with everyone. These aren't flaws so much as they are creative choices that will naturally divide opinion.

For me, however, those choices are exactly what make Mr Sleepy Man stand out. It's strange, funny, occasionally baffling and completely unafraid to be itself. That level of confidence is surprisingly refreshing.

Favourite Moment

Lost In Dreams. If you know, you know.

Favourite Boss / Character

Teefy. Weird, lovable and impossible not to smile whenever he shows up.

Who Would Enjoy This?

Fans of quirky indie games, old-school platformers and players who enjoy experiences that are willing to be unapologetically strange. If your favourite games are the ones that leave you saying "I'm not entirely sure what I just played, but I loved it", Mr Sleepy Man will probably be right up your street.
